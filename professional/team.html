He uses ART, EMDR, CBT, trauma-focused CBT, DBT, EFT, ACT, Imago, solution-focused therapy and the Gottman Method in a direct, nonjudgmental, non-shame-based style.

Read full bio of Scott Frazier

Scott Frazier, CMHC, CCTP, is a Licensed Clinical Mental Health Counselor and the co-founder of Open Book Counseling, City Weekly's Best of Utah two years running. Since beginning his career in 1998 in forensic counseling and community mental health, he has also worked in residential treatment and clinical leadership, including as Program Manager at Sierra Tucson, where he was named Employee of the Year in 2019 for strengthening suicide-prevention and client-safety systems. Earlier, he designed a juvenile re-entry program recognized by the Federal Bureau of Prisons as a best practice. Today, Scott helps children (8+), teens, adults, and couples heal from trauma, PTSD, depression, anxiety, addiction, eating disorders, and relationship strain, including clients in high-visibility roles who need discreet care. He's direct, honest, and non-shame-based, and draws on ART, EMDR, CBT, trauma-focused CBT, DBT, EFT, ACT, IFS, Imago, solution-focused therapy, and the Gottman Method. He also mentors and supervises clinicians. He holds a master's in Community Counseling from the University of Phoenix and sees clients in Draper, Bountiful, and online.

Abby Locke

ACMHC

Tough transitions, rocky relationships, and trauma in a safe, nonjudgmental space.

Read full bio of Abby Locke

Hi, I'm Abby, an Associate Clinical Mental Health Counselor (ACMHC) at Open Book Counseling. Life can leave us feeling isolated, overwhelmed, or stuck, and whatever you're facing, you don't have to face it alone. Starting therapy is a courageous first step toward healing, growth, and renewed hope. I know that opening up about hard things can feel intimidating, so I work to create a genuine, compassionate, and emotionally safe space where you will be seen, heard, and supported without judgment. I believe healing grows out of authentic connection and a trusting therapeutic relationship. My approach draws on Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), an evidence-based method for understanding how our thoughts, emotions, and behaviors shape one another. Together we can identify unhelpful thought patterns, find healthier ways to respond to difficult emotions and situations, and build practical skills for meaningful, lasting change, all tailored to your needs, goals, strengths, and experiences. My own experiences with difficult life transitions, challenging relationships, trauma, and growing up in a broken home fuel my commitment to walking with others through painful and uncertain seasons. I am well trained and work under close clinical supervision. I look forward to meeting you, hearing your story, and helping you move toward the life you want to create.
