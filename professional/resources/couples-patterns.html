The patterns couples get stuck in

Couples therapy at Open Book is counseling for couples who want to understand the patterns they get stuck in and strengthen their connection. The services list puts it in six words the site already uses: work on conflict, connection, and trust together.

The site does not name a catalog of pattern types. It describes three approaches that speak to a cycle, to conflict, and to the history behind a reaction. This draft stays with those descriptions.

The cycle

Emotionally Focused Therapy is described as mostly for couples. The work is to find the cycle you get stuck in and rebuild a secure emotional bond.

That is the pattern language on the couples page. A couple comes in already caught in something that repeats. The published aim of this approach is to find that cycle and rebuild a secure emotional bond. It is offered by Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier, and Aspen Johnson.

Conflict, friendship, and connection

The Gottman Method is a research-based approach for couples that builds friendship, manages conflict, and strengthens connection.

On the site, conflict is something the method manages, alongside friendship and connection. The same three words show up in the short service line: conflict, connection, and trust. Gottman is offered by Scott, Amber, Shanna Manjang, Aspen, and Abby Locke. Amanda Lloyd lists couples therapy and is not listed for the Gottman Method.

Hearing the history behind a reaction

Imago Relationship Therapy is a structured couples dialogue that helps partners really hear each other and the history behind their reactions.

The published idea is a set form of dialogue. Partners practice hearing each other, including the history behind a reaction, rather than only the moment in front of them. Imago is offered by Scott and Amber.

Who sees couples

All six counselors list couples therapy: Scott, Amber, Shanna, Amanda, Aspen, and Abby. The approach lists are narrower than that, as above.

Locations follow each counselor:

  • Scott and Amber: Draper and Bountiful, and telehealth.
  • Shanna: Draper and telehealth.
  • Amanda: telehealth only.
  • Aspen: Draper and telehealth.
  • Abby: Draper and Bountiful, and telehealth.

Scott's published bio says he is direct, honest, and non-shame-based, and that he draws on ART, EMDR, CBT, trauma-focused CBT, DBT, EFT, ACT, IFS, Imago, solution-focused therapy, and the Gottman Method. His work includes relationship strain. Aspen's published bio names repeating unhealthy relationship patterns and the Gottman Method for couples. Those are the pattern phrases already attached to their names. This draft does not add examples from other couples.

Family work is a separate service: counseling that brings family members together to work through conflict, change, and hard seasons. All six counselors list family therapy as well.

The approach names are also on the approaches page, with the explanations in the Skill Book.
