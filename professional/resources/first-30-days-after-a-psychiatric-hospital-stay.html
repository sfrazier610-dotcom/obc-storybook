The first 30 days after a psychiatric hospital stay

If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, 24/7). In Utah you can also use SafeUT. If it is an emergency, call 911. This website is not monitored for emergencies. More options are on the crisis page.

Coming home after a psychiatric hospital stay is a health event. The American Foundation for Suicide Prevention says people need time, rest, and support while they recover, the same way they would after any other serious health concern. You do not have to have the whole story figured out before you start getting better. (After an attempt)

The first days at home

A few ordinary things help more than they look.

  • Be gentle with yourself. You just got through a life-threatening health crisis.
  • Sleep, meals, and a little movement matter. AFSP names exercise, food, sleep, and time with people who are good for you.
  • Keep the follow-up appointments that were set before you left.
  • Take medication the way it was prescribed. The National Institute of Mental Health says not to start, stop, or change a medicine on your own, and that many antidepressants need about 4 to 8 weeks before you can tell how they are working. Sleep, appetite, and concentration often improve before mood does. (NIMH: Depression)
  • Tell one or two people you trust what you want them to do if things get hard again.

NIMH also says that some children, teens, and young adults under 25 can have an increase in suicidal thoughts or behavior when they start an antidepressant or the dose changes, especially in the first few weeks. The FDA advises that people of any age be watched closely in those weeks. If that happens, contact the prescriber, call or text 988, or call 911 if someone is in immediate danger.

A written safety plan belongs in this stretch of time. The steps are on Safety planning, step by step. A printable sheet is the SAMHSA 988 safety plan (updated April 22, 2025).

If you are coming to Open Book

Open Book's counselors work with Steffani Winter, MSN, APRN, PMHNP-BC, for clients who are leaving the hospital after trauma or a suicide attempt. The counselors provide the therapy. Steffani provides the medication management. Only she prescribes at Open Book. Family sessions are part of step-down care after a hospital or residential stay.

Hospitals and other providers can use the referral page. It helps to send a discharge summary, a current medication list, and a signed release so the therapist and Steffani can share a plan with you. Discharge planners use the main line, (385) 524-6665. There is not a separate line. Fax: (801) 207-7381. We respond to a referral within one business day. You can also request an appointment yourself. Please don't send health details by email. Asking for a time does not confirm the appointment.

After office hours, current clients contact the crisis line. Call or text 988, use SafeUT, or call 911 in an emergency. There is no on-call option.

Care for depression, including suicidal thoughts, is described on the depression page. Medication management is described on its own page.

Hours are Monday through Friday, 8am to 9pm, and Saturday, 8am to 4pm. Sunday is closed.

This page is general education. It is not a personal treatment plan and it is not medical advice.
