# Safety planning, step by step

Unpublished draft. Not linked from the site.

If you are in crisis right now, call or text [988](tel:988). In Utah you can also use [SafeUT](https://safeut.org/). If it is an emergency, call [911](tel:911). A safety plan is something you write before the worst moment, or with help during one. This website is not monitored for emergencies. More options are on the [crisis page](/crisis/).

A safety plan is a short list you make for yourself. It is not a promise that you will never feel suicidal. It is a page you can look at when thinking gets narrow.

## The six steps on the SAMHSA sheet

SAMHSA publishes a one-page safety plan, modified from Stanley and Brown, last updated April 22, 2025. The sheet asks for these six things. ([SAMHSA safety plan](https://www.samhsa.gov/resource/988/safety-plan), [PDF](https://www.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/988-safety-plan.pdf))

1. Three warning signs that a crisis may be developing. These are the thoughts, feelings, body cues, or situations that show up early for you.
2. Three internal coping strategies that can take your mind off the problem without another person. Examples people often use are a walk, a shower, music, or a slow breath. Pick ones you have actually done.
3. Three people or places that distract you, with phone numbers. This is company or a change of scene, not a deep talk.
4. People you can ask for help, with phone numbers. These are the people you would tell that you are not safe.
5. Professionals and agencies. The sheet includes your clinician, a local urgent care or emergency department with the address, and 988 by call, text, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/).
6. Two ways to make the environment safer. This is about reducing access to things that could be used to die, for as long as the risk is high. A clinician, 988, or a family member you trust can help you decide what that means in your home.

The American Foundation for Suicide Prevention lists the same kind of plan for someone recovering after an attempt: know what raises the risk, have coping steps that do not depend on other people, go to people and places that help, ask family or friends for help in a crisis, call 988, and keep the environment safe. ([After an attempt](https://afsp.org/after-an-attempt))

The official Stanley-Brown forms are at [suicidesafetyplan.com/forms](https://suicidesafetyplan.com/forms/). Individual use is allowed. This draft does not copy or change that form.

## How to use it

Write it when you are steadier, if you can. Put it where you will see it: a photo on your phone, a card in a wallet, and a copy with one person you named in step 4. Read it out loud once so the steps are familiar.

If a warning sign shows up, start at the top and move down. You can skip to 988 or 911 at any step. You do not have to finish the list first.

Review the plan after a hard week, a medication change, or a hospital stay. Warning signs and phone numbers go stale.
