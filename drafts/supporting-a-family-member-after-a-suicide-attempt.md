# Supporting a family member after a suicide attempt

Unpublished draft. Not linked from the site.

If you are in crisis, or the person you love is in crisis, call or text [988](tel:988). You can call for yourself when you are worried about someone else. In Utah you can also use [SafeUT](https://safeut.org/). If it is an emergency, call [911](tel:911). This website is not monitored for emergencies. More options are on the [crisis page](/crisis/).

An attempt is frightening for the people around it. The [988 Lifeline page for helping someone else](https://988lifeline.org/help-someone-else/) says to take care of yourself too, because supporting someone can stir up hard feelings. If it does, call 988 for yourself.

## What to say, and what to skip

988’s guidance is plain:

- Be direct. Talk about suicide in ordinary words.
- Listen. Let the person say how they feel.
- Don’t debate whether the feelings are right, and don’t lecture.
- Don’t dare them. Don’t act shocked. Don’t promise to keep a plan secret.
- You can offer hope that other options exist. Skip the quick “you’ll be fine.”
- If they have told you about a plan to hurt themselves, contact 988. Calls go to a local center that can help you find resources.

The same page lists five steps: Ask. Be there. Help keep them safe. Help them connect. Follow up.

Helping keep them safe includes reducing access to things that could be used in another attempt, such as weapons or large amounts of medication. You do not have to do that alone. 988 and the hospital team can help you think it through.

## After they come home

The American Foundation for Suicide Prevention says family and friends may be scared, confused, or angry, and may not know what to say. They may need time. Their feelings are real, and the person who made the attempt is not in charge of managing those feelings. AFSP asks families to get support for themselves and to take steps to care for the person who was hurt. ([After an attempt](https://afsp.org/after-an-attempt))

Practical help is useful: rides to appointments, meals, a quieter house, and someone who will check in later in the week, not only on the day they come home. The National Institute of Mental Health suggests the same kind of help for a person who is depressed: patience, a walk, a reminder about medicine that was prescribed, and a way to get to therapy. ([NIMH: Depression](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression))

A safety plan is a short written list the person makes, usually with a clinician. The steps are on [Safety planning, step by step](safety-planning-step-by-step.md). Ask what part they want you to hold, such as a phone number to call or a ride. Don’t rewrite their plan for them.
