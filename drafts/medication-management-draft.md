### 2. What medication management is

Psychiatric medication management is ongoing care with a licensed prescriber. It is not a one-time visit for a refill.

A typical course of care includes:

- A psychiatric evaluation of your symptoms, health history, current medications, and goals  
- A conversation about whether medication is appropriate, and about options, possible side effects, and alternatives — including therapy alone, when that is a reasonable choice  
- A prescription, when you and the prescriber decide to move forward  
- Follow-up visits to see how the medication is working, watch for side effects, and adjust the dose or change medications if needed  

People respond to medications in different ways. The first option is not always the right fit, and it can take time to judge how a medicine is working. Your prescriber should explain what to expect and should be told about side effects or problems rather than stopping a medication on your own.

Medication can be one part of a treatment plan. It is not a cure-all, and it is often most useful when it is paired with therapy and other supports. This page is general information, not medical advice. Decisions about your care belong in a visit with your prescriber.

### 3. Conditions and types of medication

Medication management may be part of care for concerns such as:

- Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)  
- Depression  
- Anxiety and panic  
- Bipolar and other mood disorders  
- Trauma-related symptoms and PTSD  
- Obsessive-compulsive symptoms  
- Sleep difficulties related to mental health  

These are examples, not a complete list, and a diagnosis is made in an evaluation — not by reading a web page.

When medication is appropriate, the types involved may include antidepressants, anti-anxiety medications, mood stabilizers, medications used for ADHD (including controlled medications when clinically appropriate), antipsychotic medications when they are indicated, and GLP-1 medications. Steffani can prescribe controlled medications when clinically appropriate, including ADHD medications. Whether a particular medicine is a good fit depends on your history, symptoms, and a clinical evaluation.

We describe medications by type, not by brand name, because the right choice is individual. For plain-language background on mental health medications, the [National Institute of Mental Health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/mental-health-medications) maintains a public overview. It is educational, not a recommendation of any specific drug.

### 7. Common questions

**What is psychiatric medication management?**  
It is ongoing care with a licensed prescriber: an evaluation, a decision about whether medication is appropriate, a prescription when you both agree to proceed, and regular follow-up to monitor how it is working and to make changes if needed. It is not only a refill appointment.

**Do I have to be a therapy client at Open Book?**  
No. Steffani can see anyone ages 8 and up. You do not need to be in therapy here, and you do not need a referral from one of our counselors. If you already work with a therapist — here or elsewhere — she can coordinate with that person when it is appropriate and you agree to it.

**Can a psychiatric nurse practitioner prescribe medication, including ADHD medication?**  
Yes. A board-certified psychiatric mental health nurse practitioner can evaluate, diagnose, and prescribe mental health medications. Steffani can prescribe controlled medications when clinically appropriate, including ADHD medications. The choice of any medication, including a stimulant or other controlled medication, depends on a clinical evaluation and applicable rules.

**How is a PMHNP different from a psychiatrist?**  
A psychiatrist is a physician (an MD or DO) who completed medical school and a psychiatry residency. A psychiatric mental health nurse practitioner (PMHNP) is an advanced practice registered nurse with graduate training in psychiatric care and national board certification. Both can evaluate mental health conditions and prescribe psychiatric medications. Steffani is a PMHNP, not a psychiatrist. People who search for a “psychiatrist near me” are often looking for a prescriber; a PMHNP may be the right fit, depending on your needs.

**What ages do you see?**  
Steffani sees children, teens, and adults, starting at age 8.

**Do you offer telehealth?**  
Yes. Medication management is available in person in Draper and Bountiful and by telehealth. For some medications, an in-person visit may be required. Steffani will explain what applies in your situation.

**What insurance do you accept?**  
We’re credentialing Steffani with the [same insurance plans we accept for therapy](https://www.openbooktherapist.com/professional/fees-insurance.html). Because credentialing is in progress, please confirm coverage with Makayla before you come in. If you plan to pay on your own, ask Makayla about fees for medication management visits. (The self-pay amounts listed for therapy on our fees page do not automatically apply to nurse practitioner visits.)

**Can I keep my current prescriber, or transfer my medications here?**  
Yes, you can ask about transferring care. Steffani will still need to complete her own evaluation before making changes or taking over prescriptions. If you prefer to stay with your current prescriber, she will not require you to switch.

**Do I need a referral?**  
No. You can request an appointment directly.

**How often will I need follow-up visits?**  
It depends on your situation. Many people are seen more often while a medication is being started or adjusted, and less often once things are stable. Your schedule is something you and Steffani will set together.
