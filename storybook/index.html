Plain version

Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing. Uses guided eye movements or taps while you briefly recall a hard memory, helping your brain re-file it so it feels less raw.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier, Amanda Lloyd, Aspen Johnson, Abby Locke

Accelerated Resolution Therapy. A short-term, eye-movement-based approach where you can change how a distressing image feels without having to talk through every detail.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy. Notice the thought-feeling-action loop and practice more helpful thoughts and behaviors.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier, Shanna Manjang, Amanda Lloyd, Aspen Johnson, Abby Locke

Trauma-Focused CBT. A structured CBT model for children, teens and caregivers recovering from trauma, with skills plus a trauma narrative.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier, Shanna Manjang, Aspen Johnson, Abby Locke

Dialectical Behavior Therapy. Skills for big emotions: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation and relationships.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier, Shanna Manjang, Amanda Lloyd, Aspen Johnson

Internal Family Systems. Get to know the 'parts' of you (the protector, the critic, the hurt part) with curiosity instead of shame.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier, Shanna Manjang, Amanda Lloyd, Aspen Johnson

Emotionally Focused Therapy. Mostly for couples. Find the cycle you get stuck in and rebuild a secure emotional bond.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier, Aspen Johnson

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy. Make room for hard feelings and take steps toward what you value.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier, Amanda Lloyd, Aspen Johnson, Abby Locke

Imago Relationship Therapy. Structured couples dialogue that helps partners really hear each other and the history behind their reactions.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier

Solution-Focused Brief Therapy. Focuses on goals, strengths and what's already working.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Shanna Manjang, Aspen Johnson

Play therapy. Children use play, their natural language, to work through feelings and experiences with a trained therapist.

Offered by: Amber Frazier, Aspen Johnson, Abby Locke

A research-based approach for couples that builds friendship, manages conflict, and strengthens connection.

Offered by: Scott Frazier, Amber Frazier, Shanna Manjang, Aspen Johnson, Abby Locke

Professional profile

What this care is

Psychiatric medication management is ongoing care with a licensed prescriber. It is not a one-time visit for a refill. A typical course includes an evaluation, a conversation about whether medication is appropriate, a prescription when you and Steffani decide to move forward, and follow-up visits. People respond differently. The first option is not always the right fit. This page is general information, not medical advice.

Only the nurse practitioner prescribes medication. Our counselors do not.

Medication types

When medication is appropriate, the types involved may include antidepressants, anti-anxiety medications, mood stabilizers, medications used for ADHD (including controlled medications when clinically appropriate), antipsychotic medications when they are indicated, and GLP-1 medications. Steffani can prescribe controlled medications when clinically appropriate, including ADHD medications. For some medications, an in-person visit may be required. She will explain what applies in your situation.

Care already offered here

  • Trauma and PTSD, including EMDR and ART, provided by our counselors, not by Steffani
  • Anxiety and depression
  • Children and teens. Medication management is for ages 8 and up.
  • Couples and family
  • Grief, shame, autism, faith transitions, burnout and high-visibility roles, and life transitions, listed by counselors on the team page

Our perinatal team is Abby Locke, Amber Frazier, and Scott Frazier, now with medication support.

Coming home from a hospital stay? We coordinate step-down care with hospitals so your medication and therapy pick up where inpatient care left off.

Why medication and therapy work better together

Medication and therapy help in different ways. Medication can ease symptoms like low mood, panic or poor sleep, which makes it easier to do the work of therapy. Therapy builds the skills, insight and healing that last after treatment ends. Research supports using them together. A review of 52 randomized trials with more than 3,600 adults found that adding therapy to antidepressant medication worked meaningfully better than medication alone for major depression, panic disorder and OCD, and the benefit lasted up to two years (Cuijpers et al., World Psychiatry, 2014). In a large trial of adults with chronic depression, 73% improved with medication plus therapy, compared with 48% with either one alone (Keller et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 2000). At Open Book, Steffani and your therapist work as one team. With your permission, they share a plan so your medication and therapy support each other, including when you're stepping down from a hospital stay. Medication isn't right for everyone, and Steffani will talk through every option with you.

Insurance and visits

We're credentialing Steffani with the same insurance plans we accept for therapy. Please confirm your benefits before your visit. Call us at (385) 524-6665 for current medication visit information.

Important information

This chapter is for general information only. It is not medical advice, a diagnosis, or a treatment recommendation, and it does not create a provider-patient relationship. Do not start, stop, or change a medication based on this website. Talk with your prescriber.

If you are in crisis, call or text 988, the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, available 24/7. In Utah, you can also use SafeUT. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 911. This website and our email are not monitored for emergencies. More resources are on our crisis page.

How the Lantern scores (fully transparent)
  • Each concern you choose that a counselor lists: +3
  • Age group listed: +2 (not publicly listed: +1, confirm)
  • Format listed: +2 (not listed: +1, confirm)
  • Each preferred approach they use: +1
